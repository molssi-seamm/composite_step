User Guide#

A composite method makes an energy from several ordinary calculations: a geometry and frequencies at one level, single points at others at that geometry, and a formula that combines them (extrapolations to the basis-set limit, additive corrections, empirical terms). The Composite plug-in describes each method as a recipe, a short YAML file, and offers every recipe as a model chemistry, SEAMM:CMP@<recipe>.

Using a composite method#

Composite methods are used through a Model Chemistry step, like any other model chemistry, by the steps that take one. For example:

SEAMM:CMP@revDSD-r2SCAN-3c-HO

The Thermochemistry step’s CBH presets are recipes: quick is r2SCAN-3c-HO and default is revDSD-r2SCAN-3c-HO, and the step records the recipe, its version and its fingerprint with the results.

The components of a recipe are ordinary calculations, each with its own model chemistry and settings fingerprint. A component already in the fragment store is reused, and one shared by two recipes is computed once; the store keeps components, never composites.

The Composite step itself, in a flowchart, does nothing yet.

The shipped recipes#

r2SCAN-3c-HO

r2SCAN-3c geometry, frequencies and energy (ORCA), with the zero-point energy scaled by 0.9899 and scaled harmonic thermal terms (“HO”).

revDSD-r2SCAN-3c-HO

r2SCAN-3c geometry and frequencies, as above, and the revDSD-PBEP86-D4(2021)/def2-TZVPP energy at that geometry.

Three recipes give accurate reference values, after W4 (Karton et al., J. Chem. Phys. 125, 144108 (2006)), on r2SCAN-3c geometries with scaled harmonic thermal terms. Each adds to a valence CCSD(T) energy the core-valence correlation (all of the outer core correlated, minus a frozen valence core, with cc-pwCVTZ) and the scalar relativistic correction (DKH2 with a point nucleus, minus the non-relativistic calculation):

CCSD(T)-F12+CV+DKH2-r2SCAN-3c-HO

The valence energy from CCSD(T)-F12D/RI with cc-pVTZ-F12 (ORCA’s F12, not Molpro’s F12b), and the relativistic correction with cc-pVTZ-DK. For H-Ar: cc-pVTZ-F12 exists for no heavier element.

CCSD(T)-CBS(TQ)+CV+DKH2-r2SCAN-3c-HO

The HF energy with cc-pVQZ and the CCSD(T) correlation extrapolated from cc-pVTZ and cc-pVQZ, and the relativistic correction with cc-pVTZ-DK. For H-Ar and Ga-Br, with all-electron basis sets.

CCSD(T)-CBS(TQ)+CV+DKH2(decontracted)-r2SCAN-3c-HO

As the previous one, but the relativistic correction uses decontracted cc-pVTZ in both halves, for species with Ca, which has no DK basis. It agrees with the cc-pVTZ-DK correction within 0.003 kJ/mol on two W4 reactions and within 0.06 kJ/mol on GeH4 -> GeH2 + H2. X2C with x2c-TZVPall was not used: on CaH2 its correction to the atomization energy differed from decontracted DKH2 by 0.82 kJ/mol.

The core-valence and relativistic corrections agree with W4’s to 0.13 and 0.01 kJ/mol on the reactions CO + 3 H2 -> CH4 + H2O and CO2 + 4 H2 -> CH4 + 2 H2O.

Recipes are named by what they contain, not by the role they play in another step, so a result labelled with a recipe stays true when presets change.

Writing a recipe#

A recipe is a YAML file:

recipe: revDSD-r2SCAN-3c-HO
version: 1
description: >-
  r2SCAN-3c geometry and frequencies (ZPE scaled), and the
  revDSD-PBEP86-D4(2021)/def2-TZVPP energy at that geometry.
reference: Santra, Sylvetsky, Martin, J. Phys. Chem. A 123, 5129 (2019)

geometry:
  level: ORCA:CMP@R2SCAN-3C
frequencies:
  scale_zpe: 0.9899
  scale_source: where the factor comes from

components:
  revdsd:
    level: ORCA:DFT@REVDSD-PBEP86-D4_2021/def2-TZVPP

combine: |
  E0 = revdsd

recipe, version, geometry and combine are required. The name may not contain :, @, / or |, which separate the parts of a model chemistry.

geometry

The level of the geometry and frequencies, as a model chemistry.

frequencies

scale_zpe, the factor for the zero-point energy and the harmonic thermal terms, and scale_source, where it comes from.

components

The single points at the recipe’s geometry, by name. Each gives its level, a complete model chemistry. The code of every component must be installed; any basis set may be given for a method the code offers.

combine

Assignments, one per line, in a small language: numbers, the names of the components, geometry (the geometry level’s own energy), the values assigned on earlier lines, the constants under empirical, + - * /, parentheses and these functions:

  • cbs_power(e_x, e_y, x, y, alpha=3) – two-point inverse-power extrapolation from the cardinal numbers x and y;

  • hf_extrapolate_exp(e_x, e_y, n, alpha) – the HF energy as E(n) = E_CBS + B (n + 1) exp(-alpha sqrt(n)), from cardinal numbers n and n + 1 (alpha 1.63 in G4; 9 in Karton and Martin’s form);

  • schwenke(e_x, e_y, factor) – Schwenke’s two-point extrapolation;

  • scale(e, factor);

  • hf_part(c) and corr_part(c) – the HF and correlation parts of a correlated component, and part(c, "name") – any named part (see Parts of an energy).

E0 must be assigned: it is the composite energy. Every value must scale like an energy, so a combination gives the same result in any unit; this is checked when the recipe is read. Empirical constants are given in hartree.

description, reference, doi, uncertainty and validation describe a recipe without changing its energy. A recipe’s fingerprint covers every other field, so a result records exactly which recipe made it.

The thermal terms follow from the geometry level: H(0 K) = E0 + s ZPE, and H(T) = E0 + s ZPE + [H(T) - E - ZPE], with s the ZPE scale factor.

Parts of an energy#

A correlated calculation reports the parts of its energy with it, so a recipe can treat them separately: extrapolate the correlation energy but not the HF energy, or scale the triples. ORCA reports them for MP2, RI-MP2, CCSD(T), DLPNO-CCSD(T) and their F12 forms:

Run

Parts

MP2

HF, MP2 correlation

RI-MP2

HF, RI-MP2 correlation

CCSD(T)

HF, CCSD correlation, (T); MP2 correlation (closed-shell RHF only)

CCSD(T)-F12D/RI

HF, CABS singles, CCSD correlation, F12 correction, (T); MP2 correlation (RHF only)

DLPNO-CCSD(T)

HF, DLPNO-MP2 correlation, DLPNO-CCSD correlation, DLPNO-(T0)

DLPNO-CCSD(T)-F12D

HF, CABS singles, DLPNO-CCSD correlation, F12 correction, DLPNO-(T0), DLPNO-MP2 correlation

Some parts are computed from others when asked for: CCSD-F12 correlation (CCSD correlation + F12 correction), HF+CABS, (T*) (the triples scaled by f, the ratio of the CCSD correlation with and without F12) and their DLPNO forms.

  • HF is the energy of the reference determinant the correlation was built on: the SCF energy, or for quasi-restricted orbitals (QRO) the QRO determinant’s energy, not the UHF one. For F12 it does not include the CABS singles.

  • (T) is never scaled. f is ORCA’s own value for canonical F12; for DLPNO-F12, where ORCA prints none, it is computed with the same definition.

  • The DLPNO correlation is everything ORCA’s total includes. ORCA’s printed “uncorrected” DLPNO-F12 correlation leaves some of it out, so the CCSD correlation is the total correlation less the F12 correction and the triples.

  • The parts of a calculation always sum to its energy, within 2e-9 E_h. If they do not, no parts are reported.

hf_part(c) is HF, and corr_part(c) is the correlation alone: the energy less HF and the CABS singles, which belong to neither. A recipe that splits an F12 component with hf_part and corr_part must use its part(c, "CABS singles"), or say omit: ["CABS singles"] for that component; otherwise it is refused when it is read. HF parts of components with different references (RHF, UHF, QRO) cannot be combined unless the recipe says allow_mixed_reference: true.

The parts belong to their calculation. They are stored with it in the fragment store, and a recipe reuses them only from the same calculation (the same settings fingerprint). A stored energy without parts, needed by a recipe that uses parts, is computed again; parts are never derived.

Frozen cores and relativistic corrections#

A component can be given:

frozen_core

The name of a set in frozen_core_definitions (the number of frozen ORBITALS for each element), none (all electrons correlated), or default (the program’s choice; not allowed in shipped recipes). A structure with an element the set does not cover is refused. ORCA counts core electrons, and its defaults are not the usual conventions for Li, Be, Na, Mg, K, Ca or Ga-Kr, so recipes give the counts explicitly. After each calculation the number of frozen orbitals is checked against the count asked for.

relativistic, nucleus

A scalar-relativistic Hamiltonian, DKH2 or X2C, with the nucleus model stated explicitly, point or finite. The shipped recipes use a point nucleus: our explicit choice; W4 does not state one.

decontract

true decontracts the orbital basis.

The shipped recipes’ sets are valence (the cc-pVnZ conventions: 1s frozen for Li-Ne, 1s-2p for Na-Ar, 1s-3p for K and Ca, 1s-3d for Ga-Kr) and inner (the cc-pwCVnZ conventions: all electrons for Li-Ne, 1s frozen for Na-Ar, 1s-2p for K and Ca and for Ga-Kr), each with its source in frozen_core_sources.

A difference of two components, a - b, must change one thing only: the method, the basis set, the frozen core, or the relativistic Hamiltonian (with its basis), so that it is the correction it is meant to be. Anything else is refused when the recipe is read.

Where recipes come from#

The shipped recipes come with the plug-in. Recipes of your own go in a recipes directory of SEAMM’s data path (the job’s data, or yours). A recipe of your own may not reuse the name and version of a shipped one.

What is not there yet#

A recipe runs anywhere a model chemistry is used: the Energy step, the Thermochemistry step’s CBH approach, and the others. A recipe that combines several calculations runs each component as one batch of its own program over all the structures, the cheapest components first. A structure that fails one component is not sent to the later ones, and its result names the component that failed. Each component’s memory and cores are its own program’s; note that a program can use more memory than it is told: ORCA’s canonical CCSD(T) with a quadruple-zeta basis used about 2.7 times its %maxcore, so size such components by the memory they actually use. Each result carries the composite record: the recipe’s fingerprint together with every component’s settings fingerprint.

Not there yet:

  • Each component needs a complete level; choosing the code from a method and a basis set is not implemented yet.

  • Only ORCA reports the parts of its energies so far.

  • Empirical terms that depend on the structure (higher-level corrections, spin-orbit tables) are not implemented; a recipe that needs them is refused.

  • Composite methods give energies only: no gradients, so they cannot be used to optimize structures or by steps that need forces.
